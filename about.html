Hi,

My name is Yedong Sh-Chen.

I study games, AI, language, emotion science, and East Asian media culture and technologies, with a particular focus on modern China and the global Sinophone world.

I received my Ph.D. from the Department of East Asian Languages and Civilizations at Harvard in May 2026, and am currently teaching at Wellesley College.

My current book project, "Chinese Video Games: World, Object, and Artificial Intelligence," provides the first empirical history of both digital gaming in China and the global circulation of Chinese games and gaming technologies, from the pixel era to the age of generative AI. It addresses key theoretical issues including world-building, artificial affect, AI ethics, and the politics of play, gender, and pleasure.

I'm also working on two side projects. One is to excavate the overlooked contributions of East Asian countries to the invention of computer graphics; the other is to trace the history of emotion science in East Asia and technologies for reproducing emotion and feelings -- what I call the “technology of Qing 情" (sentiment, emotion, feeling, affect, mood, etc.).

In my spare time, I help the Harvard-Yenching Library collect games.

I used to be a stage actor in China and off-Broadway.

Now I'm concerned about the technicity of play and role-play in the age of generative AI.
